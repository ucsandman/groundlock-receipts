## Design Context

### Users
GroundLock is for technically curious publishers, security reviewers, AI builders, journalists, public-interest technologists, and counterparties who need to check AI-generated business messages without trusting screenshots, dashboards, or a private vendor API. First-time readers may be skeptical or confused about what a "proof receipt" can actually prove.

### Brand Personality
Plain, accountable, and public-infrastructure minded. The product should feel like a civic verification tool: serious, direct, inspectable, and useful without asking the verifier to create an account.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use the existing ledger/blueprint visual language: paper surfaces, hard borders, high-contrast ink, brass/danger/accent colors, and sparse technical diagrams. Avoid glossy AI-agent dashboards, dark neon, generic SaaS hero cards, and claims that sound broader than the cryptography supports.

### Design Principles
- Lead with the dnsfs-style concept: DNS resolver-cache TXT records are the public receipt store.
- Explain the trust boundary in plain English before introducing implementation details.
- Keep every claim narrow: GroundLock proves signed provenance and integrity for one canonicalized message, not truth.
- Treat public verification as the social value: free, accountless, fail-closed, and inspectable.
- Make phishing/spam language precise: expose unsigned impersonation, but do not promise spam filtering or content safety.
